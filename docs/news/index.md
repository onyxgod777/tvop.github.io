### 🤖 Five Dollars for Ten Hours — the People Filming Themselves So Robots Can Learn Their Trade

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    A new industry has found its raw material, and the raw material is other people's working lives. **Mohammed Ashim, 22, has cut hair and trimmed beards in Bengaluru for four years; recently he has been strapping a camera to his head to record his work — it captures only his hands and fingers, with clients' faces blurred — for five or six hours a day. "The routine is simple, but you have to be disciplined about how you move. The operations team taught me that my hands always have to stay cleanly inside the frame, the lighting has to be sharp, and I can't tilt my head too abruptly while working… It's good money, but honestly, the feeling of doing something this futuristic is what keeps me excited. I never imagined my razor techniques would be useful to a global tech company"** (BBC). **He is one of hundreds of thousands of workers across the world paid to record their activities; the footage is collected by a local company, sold on to technology companies and research organisations, and used to train "physical AI" — artificial intelligence intended to control robots in the real world** (BBC).

    **The economics are stated by the people paying, and they are the story.** **Rashdan Technologies pays $5 to $10 (£3.74–£7.47) for eight to ten hours of recorded work, its co-founder and CEO Shahil Baig says: "For a lot of these people, that's meaningful… We record hands, not faces, and where multi-camera rigs do capture more of the body or environment, we blur faces out. No names, no contact details, nothing that identifies them personally goes into what we hand off." But his customers are getting more demanding: "Now clients are asking for four to six cameras rigged around a person's head to cover the entire scene, not just the hands… We pay more for that tier of data collection because of what we're asking of people"** (BBC). **Manish Agarwal of Humyn Labs, which has 96,000 data collectors across Latin America and Southeast Asia, explains why the market exists at all: "There is no existing internet archive of embodied human activity — no dataset of a kitchen being cleaned, or a car being assembled by hands that know exactly how much force to apply. That data has to be captured from scratch, in the real world, across diverse environments." Bino Paul of the Tata Institute of Social Sciences is less reassured: "By the time workers realise what they have participated in, things may have gone too far," he says, arguing that "AI cannot be viewed merely as a technical system". Textile worker Mohammed Arman, ten years in the trade: "For me, it's simple. This is survival. I have a family back home in Bihar… If someone offers me extra money to wear a camera while I do the exact same work I've been doing for 10 years, I'm not going to say no"** (BBC).

??? warning "Projected Effect (if cause persists)"

    - **Consent under economic pressure is formally valid and substantively empty** — "I'm not going to say no" is not a preference, it is a budget; the direct effect is a signature; the compound consequence is that the ethical design of the industry — blurred faces, no names — answers the question of privacy while leaving the question of power untouched, so a consent regime can be perfectly compliant and still extract
    - **The five-camera tier is the trajectory, not an anomaly** — the direct effect is a wider frame and a higher fee; the compound consequence is that every increment of value is bought by widening what is recorded — hands, then body, then the room — and there is no natural stopping point, because the marginal customer always wants the scene and the marginal worker always needs the money
    - **The work being recorded is the work being replaced** — the direct effect is a dataset of haircuts, stitching and assembly; the compound consequence is that the worker is paid to make his own skill generic — extracted, it stops commanding a premium, and a trade that took ten years to learn becomes a training input that took eight hours to film; Ashim's pride at being "useful to a global tech company" is the mechanism described from the inside
    - **Because no archive exists, the first movers are enclosing a commons** — the direct effect is that physical-AI labs must buy embodiment where labour is cheap; the compound consequence is that human motion becomes a mined resource with the same geography as every other extractive industry, and the sourcing logic is explicit: Indian homes are a proxy for American ones, so the bodies that will not own the robots supply the data that trains them
    - **The hinge is whether a data contract carries a residual** — the moment embodied-data work is classified in labour law, with a floor rate, injury cover and a right to withdraw; the moment contracts carry a royalty or a collective licence so the worker shares in what the dataset earns; the moment cameras stay opt-in with recordings deleted on request; and the moment buyers publish sourcing standards and audit them — the chain can be broken. §19 does not legislate; it locates the check where the industry has none.

    *"If you heed yourselves inwardly, then you will be warned through your admonishment (conscience) against all inequitableness and against all unfairness (irresponsibility), as well as against all unrighteousness, so that you can then decide what you must do and what you must leave undone."* — *Goblet of the Truth*, Chapter 19, Verse 23

    **The hinge:** The cause is not the camera and it is not the robot. It is the choice — by the data collectors and their clients, whether a worker's recorded motion is bought outright or licensed with a residual; by the governments now being asked to regulate, whether embodied-data labour is given a legal category with a floor rate and a right of withdrawal; by the robotics labs, whether sourcing standards are published and audited or kept as a procurement detail; and by the workers themselves, whether the extra money is taken as an individual transaction or negotiated as a trade. §19 places the check inside the person — conscience warns against inequitableness before any regulator does — and this is an industry in which every party already knows the terms and has decided they are acceptable. Whether the next decade of robotics is built on a paid-once underclass or on licensed, credited labour is not fixed by the technology; it is a decision held by named founders, named buyers and named ministers, and one that a different choice could still make.

## The Pattern

Look across these five stories. Though they span **70,000 police deployed across France and a 19th-century town hall burned in Limoges, as a high-school protest over staffing widens into a revolt against the cost of living six months before a presidential election**; **a US intelligence chief's memo saying Iranian plotting against Americans has reached "advanced stages", embassy staff withdrawn from Saudi Arabia, RAF Typhoons sent to defend Saudi skies and 21 killed in Houthi airport attacks since 1 October**; **a 50% tariff lifted from plywood, paper and refrigeration equipment two weeks before the midterms, at the request of one senator in one close race, with new tariffs imposed on carpets in the same announcement**; **Kenya's first Ebola outbreak reaching its second death — a 65-year-old woman, seven patients in isolation, three of them health workers and one a cleaner**; and **a barber in Bengaluru paid five to ten dollars for eight to ten hours of head-camera footage that will train a robot to cut hair** — the common structure is unmistakable.

**In all five, the event described as news is the *effect*, and the cause is upstream, human and named: an authority that cannot draw up an allocation plan and answers the street with 70,000 officers; a war that was never concluded being escalated one level up the chain of command; a levy granted as a favour to an electoral map; a contact list that has not yet been completed; and a contract that buys a working life outright for the price of a day.** None of these is fate, and none is weather.

Two of them are the same mechanism at different scales. **France and Canada are both cases of a charge imposed by an authority that has lost the capacity to justify it.** In France the charge is a tax burden the public was never asked to approve and a budget no majority will sign; in North America it is a 50% levy that turns out to be negotiable by one senator whose state grows blueberries. §3:104 and §2:388 describe the same defect from two sides: the authority sets the charge, the people do not decide it, and the disadvantaged bear the loss. The difference is only that France's version produces a burned town hall and Canada's produces a deferred packing-house upgrade.

Two more share an evidentiary problem that has become characteristic of this page. **Yemen's dead are counted by two health ministries, and Kenya's cases are counted as a cluster of eight** — in both, the number that matters is the one nobody is obliged to publish. The Houthi-run ministry can say 155 civilians have died since July; Riyadh can say 21 have died in airport attacks; neither figure can be checked by the other, and in a war with no neutral enumerator the dead become arguments. Kenya's ministry, by contrast, is publishing its denominator — one cluster, eight cases, three health workers, one cleaner — which is exactly what makes its fortnight a deadline rather than a reassurance. **Transparency is not a courtesy in either case; it is the mechanism by which the effect can still be changed.**

The fifth will be the least visible in ten years and the most consequential. **The AI-labour story contains no violence and no emergency, and that is why it will run longest.** A worker is paid once for a recording of a skill that took a decade to learn; the recording is permanent, the payment is not; and the only party who can see the whole ledger is the buyer. Bino Paul's warning — "by the time workers realise what they have participated in, things may have gone too far" — describes a cause that accumulates quietly, and it has the same structure as the peat fires this page recorded yesterday: a slow cause, a fast effect, and an interval in which nothing appears to be happening.
