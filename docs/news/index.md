### 🇩🇪 Thirty-Four Cities and a Gun Shop in Frankfurt

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    **Germany's youth movement arrived on Saturday and left a shopping street in Frankfurt without windows.** The campaign group **"Gen Z regelt"** ("Gen Z takes charge") organised demonstrations in **34 cities** including Berlin, Frankfurt and Munich against **underfunded schools, the cost of living, cuts to education, social services, culture and healthcare, and military conscription** — the same bundle of demands that has carried French students into a third weekend of protest. During the day the crowds were large and largely peaceful; **Frankfurt, Munich and Berlin police put attendance at up to 18,000**, roughly **half of what organisers had estimated**, with German media reporting tens of thousands nationally (BBC, Reuters, AFP). **After dark it turned.** In Frankfurt — the financial hub — **at least two stores were looted and several more damaged**, with stones, fireworks and petrol bombs thrown at police, who answered with **water cannon and pepper spray**; a large section of the main shopping street was closed; **a gun shop's windows were smashed and two weapons seized after an arrest**, with a sportswear shop and a perfume store also targeted (Bild, per AFP); "many arrests" were made (BBC). In **Berlin** a smaller evening demonstration not organised by Gen Z regelt left **27 officers injured** and up to **56 people temporarily detained**; in **Hamburg** people dressed in black set off fireworks and burned barricades, with public broadcaster NDR reporting **120 arrests**; in **Cologne** officers were attacked near the main station with bottles and a thrown bicycle. Police said the situation in Frankfurt calmed overnight into Sunday.

    **Both the movement and the state immediately named a third party.** A Gen Z regelt spokesperson, **Lina Friedrich**, said the demonstrations were "to fight for peace and freedom, rather than for war and coercion", that "what matters most to us is better support for the health and education systems", and that the group opposes compulsory military service and wants "a fairer distribution of the burden"; the movement **explicitly rejected violence**. Hesse's interior minister **Roman Poseck** said **"around 100 clearly identified left-wing extremists"** had infiltrated the Frankfurt protest, called the ending "shameful", and reached for the neighbouring precedent: *"We all have those images from France in our minds — looting, burning houses, burning police vehicles, and attacks on police officers… France must not be held up as a model in this regard."* Cologne's police chief **Johannes Hermanns** said it was "deeply saddening that young people who wanted to peacefully demonstrate for their future were infiltrated today by extremists who had nothing but violence in mind." The comparison was not rhetorical: **more than 320,000 people** demonstrated across France the same day, with **over 500 arrests** after clashes in Toulouse, Lyon and Marseille and an officer injured by a Molotov cocktail; there were clashes in **Rome and Turin**, and Portuguese police cleared a new tent camp in a protest against a rental bill in a country where house prices have **more than doubled in a decade**. Youth-led cost-of-living protests have now run in **France, Belgium, Ireland, Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece, Serbia and Albania** this year (BBC).

### 🇵🇱 Two Presidents of One Tribunal

???+ danger "Observed Cause"

## The Pattern

Look across these five. Though they span **a war whose supreme leader has just declared that his response will have no geographic boundary, negotiated against a two-day plan to reopen a strait**; **an Israeli election whose 19 Arab-held seats must now recommend a prime minister and are simultaneously being legislated around**; **a German autumn in which 18,000 demonstrators produced a hundred named extremists and one looted gun shop**; **a Polish courthouse with two lawful claimants to its presidency and riot police at the door**; and **a $6.3 billion theft in which the thief is a state, the insurer is the victim, and the recovery is a press release** — the common structure is not the document this week. It is **the count**.

**In all five, the contested object is a number that one party asserts and another party must verify.** 155 inbound and 122 outbound transits is UKMTO's count; "six tankers damaged by mines" is Iran's. 18,000 demonstrators is three police forces' count and roughly half the organisers'. "Around 100 left-wing extremists" is one interior minister's. Nineteen seats is a certified count that one bloc treats as an infiltration. 90% is Binance's own number, and the remaining 10% is the part that is illiquid. **Yesterday's pattern was that the mechanism had migrated into the paperwork. Today the paper has become a tally, and the question is who is entitled to keep it** — because a count that cannot be agreed cannot be enforced, and every one of these five stories has now reached the point where the argument is no longer about the event but about the arithmetic.

**And in each case the party keeping the tally is the party with an interest in it.** Iran's state television counts the hulls. Hesse's interior ministry counts the extremists. Likud counts the Arab seats as foreign. The government counts Święczkowski as the lawful president, and the tribunal's website counts him as suspended. In four of the five, a neutral ledger is available and the parties are choosing to argue about the number instead of reading it.

**Two of these are one mechanism at different scales: a claim of protection that has never been tested.** "Maximum deterrence" through unlimited response is untested until something is struck, and the evidence so far — transits 70% below pre-war, a new hull-attached charge method, six claimed tanker losses — is that the threat has already been exercised without producing deterrence. **And two are one mechanism at different scales: an institution whose authority is asserted by the party that needs it.** Poland's tribunal has two presidents because each was installed by a different majority and neither will submit to a court above itself. Israel's 19 seats are legitimate to everyone who counts them, and illegitimate to the bloc that needs them to be — until the moment a workaround makes them unnecessary. **In both, the institution is being saved by the party that is damaging it**, and the saving operation is itself the damage: a court entered through a police cordon, a community invited to recommend a prime minister it will not sit with.
