### 🇮🇷 The Limit That Was Never a Treaty

### 🇵🇱 Three Minutes, Six Dead, and a House Two Hundred Kilometres Away

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    **The attack was over in three minutes, and the investigation began two hundred kilometres away.** Shortly before **15:00 local time on Saturday 7 November**, a man drove into **Main Market Square in Kraków** — the ornate centre of Poland's second city and one of the country's principal tourist squares — and fired *"at random people from his car"*, according to police.     **Six people were killed — four women and two men.** Three were Polish, one was **British**, and two have not been identified. **Sixteen people were taken to hospital**, among them five foreign nationals from **Austria, Germany, Portugal, Russia and Britain**, two police officers and the attacker. The suspect is a **25-year-old Polish man** in military-style clothing and equipment, armed with a military-grade weapon, described by a government minister as **"ultra-nationalist"**; the government said he was **not a member of the armed forces**, and more weapons and ammunition were found in his car. **Senior Constable Jakub Kulik**, shot three times while making the arrest, was in a very critical condition; **Senior Constable Artur Morański** was expected to recover. A man who kills his family and then drives two hours to a tourist square has a sequence, not an impulse. ### 🌍 Twelve Days for a Limit Already Crossed

???+ danger "Observed Cause"

## The Pattern

Look across these five — **a 28-day mandate with no coalition terms**, **more than 30 ballistic missiles fired at a grid two days before a table**, **a range limit lifted by a press conference**, **six people shot in three minutes in a city square**, and **twelve days of climate negotiations for a threshold already crossed** — and the structure is not the proxy, as it was last week. It is **the limit**: who declares it, who removes it, and what actually enforces it.

**Every one of these stories is about a boundary that turned out to be a statement.** Iran's 2,000-kilometre cap was never a treaty and was withdrawn in one sentence, so nothing physically changed and everything politically did. Israel's 67 recommendations ran into a limit of 61 seats that is not a limit but a veto structure held by the smallest parties. Ukraine's grid has a tolerance — a number of substations, a number of hours, a temperature — and that tolerance, not the front line, is what the next package is aimed at. Kraków had a limit of three minutes, and it was enforced by two constables with pistols. And 1.5°C was the one limit in the set that had a physical enforcer — the atmosphere — which is why it is the only one already crossed while the other four are still being argued about.

**The tell is that in every case the limit which was announced was not the limit that operated.** A mandate of 28 days is not a measure of how long coalition-building takes; a declared missile range is not a measure of what can be built; a mourning period is not a measure of how long a country will remember; a twelve-day conference is not a measure of how much can be agreed. The limits that actually operated were mechanical and unglamorous — a quorum, a switchyard, a response time, a radiative balance — and in four of the five nobody had published them, which is exactly why an announcement could stand in for them.

**And the week's most instructive coincidence is that the enforcement arrived, twice, from below the level at which decisions are made.** In Kraków it was a senior constable who closed a three-minute window. In Kyiv it was a repair crew that restored water and reconnected 60% of the heating before the next package loaded. Neither was asked to do it by a summit, a mandate or a range announcement, and both produced a measurable outcome inside a day. Compare that with the four documents meant to constrain events this week, none of which did: the seven conditions, the coalition count, the IAEA's continuity of knowledge, and the Paris temperature goal.

What has dropped away is instructive. **Paraguay moves to the case register**: Santiago Peña has been charged with corruption and appeared before prosecutors, and the compensation promise still has no funding line, so the story's next milestone is a court date rather than a development. **The European hoax campaign changed shape rather than escalating**: this week's attribution arrived in a different national file — Italy's foreign minister says hackers published his personal data on the dark web because of his country's support for Ukraine, and the prime minister blamed pro-Russian hackers — which confirms the diagnosis without producing the attribution standard this page asked for, so it stays held. **Yemen is escalating and is not in this cycle's five**: Houthi-linked media report at least 33 killed in a Saudi strike east of Sanaa on Saturday, with the toll disputed between Houthi health officials and the group's own news agency, the Saudi-led coalition declining to comment, and the UN counting more than 1,600 dead and 300,000 displaced since fighting resumed — that file is now large enough to deserve its own section rather than a sentence. And **the AI sell-off has closed as a cause**: markets are shut for the weekend and the repricing now waits on a bill nobody has drafted.
