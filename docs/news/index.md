### 💶 EU–China Trade Talks Enter the "Last Chance Saloon": Negotiators Meet in Beijing Days After Paris and Berlin Arm the Commission

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    Yesterday this page recorded the Franco-German push to give Brussels a "trade bazooka." The meeting comes days after German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and French President Emmanuel Macron sent a joint letter and position paper to Commission President Ursula von der Leyen urging the EU to adopt powers that could restrict — or "completely cut off" — imports from countries seen as creating "severe and systematic distortions" in the single market, letting the Commission act "within days" rather than the months the existing Anti-Coercion Instrument requires. The backdrop is structural: an EU goods deficit with China of roughly €360 billion last year, and, on the Chinese side, a NYT report that "huge tax breaks and a weak currency sustain exports" while widening the government's own budget deficit.

??? warning "Projected Effect (if cause persists)"

    - **A deficit rooted in tax breaks and a weak currency cannot be closed from outside** — the direct effect of Chinese overcapacity being sustained by subsidies and a managed exchange rate is that European tariffs can change the price of entry but not the structure behind it; the compound consequence is that a durable fix requires China's own rebalancing, which its widening budget deficit makes harder and less likely — so Brussels is pressing for a remedy its counterpart is not currently able to deliver
    - **A deterrent most useful when brandished is most dangerous when used** — the direct effect of arming the Commission to restrict market access "within days" is that trade retaliation no longer needs the slow consent of twenty-seven governments; the compound consequence is that the tool tempts first use precisely because it is fast, and each use invites a counter-use — so the two blocs move from managing a deficit to trading blows, and the deficit grows harder, not easier, to fix
    - **Weaponised interdependence cuts both ways, and Europe is the more exposed** — the direct effect of targeting Chinese supply chains is that Europe also depends heavily on Chinese critical raw materials, components and demand for its exports — its carmakers, chemicals and machinery; the compound consequence is that a decisive European measure can be answered by a decisive Chinese shortage, so a policy meant to protect European industry can idle the very factories it aims to defend

    *"Consider that you shall not be raised above your desires (standing above your own wishes), but that you shall proceed in modesty and not demand the gain (achievement) of people of your kind (next ones), neither their possessions and wealth nor their woman or man."* — *Goblet of the Truth*, Chapter 7, Verse 141

    **The hinge:** The cause is not the deficit and it is not the tariff.
