## The Pattern

Look across these five stories. Though they span **a supertanker burning on a mine in the Strait of Hormuz and two tankers hit off Jubail as attacks on shipping reach record highs**; **Trump waiving the Russia sanctions he signed into law while Ukraine's warning feed and satellite bandwidth are throttled**; **six hundred demonstrations and 200,000 expected across France as the Yellow Vests join the students**; **Venezuela withdrawing from the International Criminal Court while the campaign it was investigating is still being counted**; and **Brazil cutting Amazon deforestation by 41.8% — the largest drop on record — ten days before a run-off that could reverse it** — the common structure is unmistakable.

**In every one of the five, the event described as news is the *effect*. The cause is upstream, human and named: a decision, by a militia and its patron to move the war onto the water, and by a coalition to answer each sinking with the next strike rather than a demining corridor; a decision, by a government to suspend a law it wrote and to treat a defender's early warning as a bargaining chip; a decision, by a state to fund schools as a residual and to police a crowd with weapons whose last use cost a child a hand; a decision, by a government under investigation to leave the court rather than answer it, and by the court's members to let it; and a decision, by an electorate and its institutions whether enforcement of forest law survives a change of government.**

In every case, the human cause is **a choice** — and therefore, the effect is **changeable**. The Brazilian figure is the proof this page keeps pointing to: the same forest, the same chainsaws, the same international market — and a 41.8% drop within three years because the cause was changed. What changed there was not the weather; it was the will to enforce.
