### 🤖 Fifty Degrees in the Freezer

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    **Somebody turned the freezers up, in seven countries, overnight.** On Monday **Delhaize** confirmed that around **40 supermarkets in Belgium and Luxembourg** were affected, with a smaller number hit in **Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia and Finland**. **Stephan Moelans**, CEO of **Sabcobel**, which operates Delhaize's systems, told **Belga** the incident was a **cyber-attack** and that the company had been "working all night and morning to disconnect the affected units from the network". Temperatures in several shops reportedly rose to as high as **50C (122F)**. One Delhaize branch in **Hoeilaart**, Flanders, told **Het Laatste Nieuws** it had lost **€100,000** of produce — before counting the losses from closing. Millions of euros of food had already been thrown away in October, local media reported at the time.

    **The vector is the maintenance contract.** Most supermarkets across Europe are **linked to the internet**, because maintenance and troubleshooting are done remotely. Moelans said that since the **French attack in early October** the company had been working to "protect our customers fridges against this vulnerability" — but that the units needed **physical modifications, not just software updates**, and that "the simplest solution is to manage the fridges over a secure internet connection. ## The Pattern

Look across these five. Though they span **an island bombed the day before a vote**; **twenty-nine people killed at a weekly aid distribution and a truce both armies admit they used**; **53 seats, 48 seats and 19 that no one will name**; **two countries struck off a duty-free list by letter**; and **a continent's freezers set to fifty degrees** — the common structure this week is not a clock and not a document. It is **the audience**.

**In all five, the act is calibrated to a spectator rather than aimed at an adversary.** The strikes on **Qeshm** came "ahead of the midterm elections", with the President saying "no rush" to one outlet and "one hour" to another, and a former ally reported to be asking for a second wave *after* the vote. The **Komyshuvakha** strike landed a week before **Abu Dhabi**, on a village whose aid day was public knowledge, and Russian state television explained the Oleshky truce as a "success story ahead of the elections for the US president". **Herzog's 42 days** exist to be watched by two blocs who each want the other blamed for the outcome. **AGOA's** removal is announced by letter to Congress and justified by an accusation no court has tested. And the freezers were set to a number — **50C** — chosen so that a shop manager in Sjöbo would post it. **In each case the effect is produced in the mind of a third party: the voter, the mediator, the president, the base, the customer.**

**That is a weaker cause than it looks, and it fails in a specific way.** A cause aimed at an audience must be *legible* to that audience, so it must be simple — and simple causes cannot carry the detail that makes them work. A strike timed for a Tuesday cannot also be a strategy. A truce kept for a photograph cannot also be a mechanism. A sanction aimed at a domestic accusation cannot also be a negotiating position. A membership refused on principle cannot also be a coalition. **Each of these five is being conducted so that someone watching will draw a conclusion, which means each is being conducted in a form that cannot bear the weight of the thing it is actually doing.**

**Two of these are the same mechanism at two scales: the demonstration replaces the operation.** Fifty degrees in a supermarket freezer and one hour's notice for a mountain in Iran are the same instrument — a signal whose purpose is to be understood, priced so that the cost of reading it is borne by someone else. The hacker pays nothing; the shopkeeper loses €100,000 and the customer loses the food. The strike costs an aircraft's risk; the village loses twenty-nine people and the region loses its aid day. **When the point of an act is that it be seen, the damage is a by-product booked to whoever cannot refuse it.**

**And two are the same mechanism at two scales: a list that is really a verdict.** AGOA's eligibility review and Israel's coalition arithmetic both present themselves as procedures with criteria — trade barriers, corruption, workers' rights; 53, 48, 19, 61 — and both are in fact decisions taken before the procedure, with the criteria selected afterwards to fit. The 19 seats cannot be named because naming them *is* the decision; the South African barriers cannot be negotiated because the removal *is* the decision. **A procedure whose conclusion precedes it is not a procedure. It is a verdict with minutes.**

And the **Kherson model** is now closed as a question: the truce worked, both sides used it, the Kremlin has called it an isolated case — which is the clearest thing said about it.
