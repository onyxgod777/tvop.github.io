Though they span **Houthi missiles reaching Aramco's Dhahran headquarters and being intercepted over Riyadh, as the IMF warns of spillovers "for everybody, everywhere"**; **Russian strikes leaving 600,000 Ukrainians without power while Starlink speed limits disrupt battlefield missions and 12 senators try to reverse the diesel deal**; **Indonesia's worst fire season in a decade choking Singapore and Malaysia with toxic haze as 26 companies face suspended licences**; **China's exports surging 36% with its trade surplus running toward a trillion dollars**; and **MI5 warning that state threats have quadrupled and AI is "supercharging" the contest while the Kremlin is "scrambling to pay criminals"** — the common structure is unmistakable.

**In every one of the five, the event described as news is the *effect*. The cause is upstream, human and named: a decision, by the Houthis who fire on oil installations and by a coalition that answers each strike with another, to keep a decade-old war on the escalatory ladder rather than return it to a table; a decision, by Washington to trade a defender's early warning and battlefield link for an announced "energy ceasefire" that the aggressor rejects, and by a private company to reconfigure a war's communications at a political moment; a decision, by named companies to clear a concession by fire because it is cheapest, and by the regulators and markets that make that calculus worth making; a decision, by a state to subsidise exports toward a trillion-dollar surplus without raising its own households' purchasing power, and by its partners to answer with tariffs rather than rebalance; and a decision, by a state that has lost its spies to buy criminals, and by an industry to release capability faster than anyone governs it.**

In every case, the human cause is **a choice** — and therefore, the effect is **changeable**.
