On this page, we track **current world events as observed causes** and project their **necessary effects** if those causes persist unchanged. This is not fortune-telling. It is **causal inference** — the same principle that lets a physicist predict a trajectory from position and velocity. See the cause, know the effect. Change the cause, and the effect dissolves.

### 🇨🇦 Canada Fires Back — Counter-Tariffs Up to 50% as the U.S.–Canada Trade War Hardens Into a Continental Escalation

???+ danger "Observed Cause"

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne called the response "proportionate" and "strategic," and pledged an additional C$7.5bn to support workers and businesses hit by the U.S. tariffs. The Canadian levies take effect on **8 September** (BBC).

Polls show a majority of Canadians support holding firm — a public posture that makes backing down harder for both governments.

    - **Energy, minerals and autos remain the tripwire beneath the trade war** — the effect of a dispute that could still turn to Canadian oil, gas, electricity, potash and critical minerals, and to the auto sector whose parts cross the border many times before a finished vehicle, is a confrontation far graver than tariffs on tuna and furniture, with ripple effects through US fuel, fertilizer and food prices

???+ danger "Observed Cause"

    - **Yet both instruments are choices** — the moment Washington wields its leverage toward a real agreement, or Tehran accepts the reopening it is already negotiating, the effect transforms from a deepening siege and oil shock toward a managed re-opening of the world's most vital waterway

The flood, reportedly caused by excessive snow melting into the **Bhote Koshi river** flowing from Tibet into Nepal, has closed the main road to Kathmandu and cut communications and power across the region.     The cause is a **climate-intensified natural force amplified by the conditions human activity has set**: a fast-warming Himalaya shedding snow and glacial melt into a steep river valley shared by Nepal and the Chinese territory of Tibet, in a region where last year a glacier-burst flood killed 18 people.
