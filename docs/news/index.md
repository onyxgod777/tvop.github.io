### 🌍 The Verb and the Bracket

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    **COP31 has one day left to decide whether the central cause of the crisis is named in the document that responds to it.** The Antalya conference — **9 to 20 November**, co-hosted by **Türkiye** and **Australia** — closes on **Friday**, and the fight is over the **cover decision**, the political statement that sums up the summit. COP31 President of Negotiations **Chris Bowen**: *"It won't be the final draft. There are elements in this draft that not all parties will agree on. That is a given."* At least one more text is expected before Friday's gavel.

    **The opposition is organised on both sides of the sentence.** The **EU's** climate envoy **Wopke Hoekstra** said the draft *"does not go in the right direction and needs significant change"* and warned it could make the talks *"untenable."* A bloc of large developing countries including **China, India and Saudi Arabia** said the text crosses its red lines, objecting to the electrification target, the fossil-fuel roadmap and rapid methane cuts; the **Arab Group and Russia** rejected the draft outright. Small island states want the opposite — **Antigua and Barbuda's** environment minister **Michael Joseph**: *"We would love to see a commitment that calls on the phasing out of fossil fuels,"* while conceding consensus *"might be very, very difficult."* The precedent is not encouraging: last year's **Belém** summit ended with **no mention of fossil fuels** in its final text after producer states blocked the language, and temperatures have since gone **back above 1.5 °C** in recent data. The presidency's own additions point elsewhere — an **Antalya Green Industrialisation Compact** and an **Antalya Artificial Intelligence Pledge** developed with the **ITU**, launched as a Harvard study found **US hyperscale data centres emit as much CO₂ as Denmark**.

### ⚖️ The First State

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    **The International Court of Justice is delivering the first verdict in which a state stands accused of genocide — and the case exists because no other court could hear it.** On Thursday the **ICJ** rules on the case filed by **The Gambia** against **Myanmar** in **November 2019**, backed by the **Organisation of Islamic Co-operation**, over the military's **"clearance operations"** in **Rakhine State** in **August and September 2017**, when hundreds of thousands of Rohingya were driven into Bangladesh. The Gambia's argument was a novelty in international law: that every state party to the **1948 Genocide Convention** has standing to enforce it, even without being directly affected. Myanmar contested standing; the court ruled The Gambia eligible. **Myanmar is not a party to the International Criminal Court**, which is why the case was brought at the ICJ at all — a court built in 1945 to settle disputes between states, not to try crimes. In the years since the Convention was signed, dozens of individuals have been convicted of genocide, in Rwanda and the former Yugoslavia among others; **no state has ever been found guilty by an international court**.

    **The evidentiary bar is the story, and the record is unusually specific.** Myanmar denies genocide, calling the operations a response to terrorist attacks by Rohingya militants. A **2018 UN fact-finding mission**, based on hundreds of interviews, found the army's tactics *"grossly disproportionate to actual security threats"* and said senior military figures should be investigated for genocide. When Bosnia brought a case against Serbia in **1993**, the court took **14 years** to find that the Srebrenica massacre was genocide but that Serbian state intent was not proven. **Aung San Suu Kyi** — then head of the elected government, with limited control over the military — chose to defend Myanmar in person at The Hague, a decision that dismayed her admirers; since the **2021 coup** the defence has been run by a team appointed by the military, now led by **Min Aung Hlaing**, the general responsible for the 2017 operations. The Gambia's counsel **Arsalan Suleman** built the intent case on the **1982 citizenship law** and *"the tightening noose of discriminatory laws that restricted access to education, healthcare, livelihoods, and travel — all of which made life really impossible for the Rohingya."* There is **no right of appeal** against an ICJ verdict. There is also **no penalty** attached to one.

## The Pattern

Read the five together — **a war whose missiles are calibrated to miss**, **a summit that produced a paragraph about petroleum and courts instead of the war it was convened over**, **a climate text one day from its deadline with the operative verb still contested**, **a $1.5 trillion listing on $104 billion of losses**, and **the first state ever found to have committed genocide, by a court with no power to impose anything** — and the common structure is not escalation, collapse, or even management. It is **the instrument without the operative clause**.

**Each of these is a form that has been completed while its operative content was removed.** The strikes are real and named the right targets, but they were calibrated to arrive nowhere, so the act carries all the signalling value of an attack and none of the consequence. The summit has a joint statement, a photograph and a paragraph on petroleum supply, and no sentence about the war it was called to address. The cover decision has 18 pages, a presidency, a deadline, an electrification target and an adaptation figure, and the clause that names the cause is bracketed. The listing has a record $81 billion, a $1.5 trillion valuation and a ten-times oversubscribed book, and no earnings. The verdict carries the gravest finding a court can make, and no penalty attached to it.

**The removal is not an accident of drafting. It is the most defensible thing each actor could have done.** Iran can signal without forcing a US response. Three powers can meet without committing to a settlement. A presidency can produce a text no bloc will block. A company can raise capital it needs before it knows whether the business works. A court can find the crime while leaving enforcement to the politics that produced it. In each case the operative clause was removed because the clause is where the *accounting* would begin — and every one of these actors had a rational reason to defer the accounting by one more cycle.

**But a form without its operative clause does not stay neutral. It becomes the precedent for the next form.** A missile calibrated to miss sets the band for the next exchange, so the war acquires a grammar and stops requiring decisions. A communiqué that discusses a war without its victim becomes the template for the settlement, and that settlement will be delivered rather than agreed. A bracketed verb becomes next year's baseline, so each conference negotiates down from the last one's weakest paragraph. A valuation without earnings transfers risk into every index fund and makes the correction a public event. A verdict without a penalty teaches that the most serious finding available costs nothing — a lesson other governments are already positioned to learn.

**And the cost still lands at the point of failure rather than the point of decision.** A Kuwaiti air-defence crew firing interceptors over its own capital at drones meant to land nowhere. An Odesan household under a drone that did not miss. A small-island minister conceding that consensus "might be very, very difficult" while his coastline is surveyed. A retail investor taking the tenth tranche of a ten-times-oversubscribed book. A Rohingya family watching a screen in a camp where the funding is expiring. In each case the decision was rational where it was made, the deferral was defensible on its own terms, and the effect arrives on schedule — because the clause that would have forced someone to name the date was the clause that was removed.
