## The Pattern

Look across these five stories. Though they span **800,000 Yemenis cut off from lifesaving aid and Taiz besieged, with a seven-week-old pair of twins in an Aden ward and a family that walked three days to get there**; **43 documented attacks on hospitals in el-Fasher, 55 medics killed and 10 disappeared, with the UN human rights chief saying they may amount to war crimes**; **1.9 million hectares burned in Indonesia, 277,000 respiratory cases and protests spreading as the haze reaches Singapore, Malaysia and the Philippines**; **32 UK government services offline and a garage in Chepstow writing MOT certificates by hand after AI found critical flaws in PHP**; and **a magnitude 7.0 quake at 4,800m in Tibet that killed 16, collapsed 339 homes and left 12,400 people relocated in subzero cold** — the common structure is unmistakable.

**In four of the five, the event described as news is the *effect*, and the cause is upstream, human and named: a decision, by the Houthis and by the government and Riyadh, to fight over the roads into Taiz rather than to keep them open; a decision, by an armed force, that a hospital is the cornerstone of a city and therefore a target; a decision, by landowners and by a state that will not publish who owns the land, that burning is cheaper than clearing; and the absence of a decision, by anyone, to fund the maintenance of the code under a third of the web. In the fifth — Tibet — the immediate cause is a fault and not a choice, which is why it belongs here: it is the control case. The earth moved on its own; everything that determined how many died when it stopped moving was built, permitted, funded and inspected by people.**

Two of these five are accumulated effects of decisions taken long ago and only now counted: 43 attacks catalogued across eighteen months, and a flaw that sat in the foundation of the web through a decade of review that did not find it. Two were taken deliberately this week by people who knew what they were choosing: the encirclement of Taiz and the shutdown of thirty-two government services. One is a structural inheritance — the masonry and timber of a high-altitude county — that no one this week decided and everyone this week inherited.

In every case, the human cause is **a choice** — and therefore, the effect is **changeable**. Four of these five moved within twenty-four hours: Taiz went from contested to cut off, the el-Fasher chronology went from allegation to documented pattern, the PHP event went from a code advisory to thirty-two government services and a stopped MOT queue, and Tibet's toll went from unknown to sixteen with 400 aftershocks still counting. The one that has not moved is the one that would cost the most to move: a land register, a building code, a funded maintainer — the slow causes, which is precisely why they are the ones that must be decided early.

**The environment thread is now unmistakably one system, and this page will not file it under three separate headings.** The same Super El Niño that dried the Indonesian peat to the point of ignition produced Typhoon Champi — the strongest storm on the planet this year at 185mph sustained, Japan's thirtieth typhoon of 2026 and the first time since 2013 that the country has seen so many — which shut schools, offices and a $5tn stock exchange on Friday and injured 171 people in Taiwan. Three continents, one season, one signature. The attribution argument that protects a concession holder in Riau is the same argument that delays a heat-mortality register in Madrid: weather is the multiplier, and the decision is always the one made before the weather.

**The two wars on this page are converging on the same target, and it is not a military one.** Yemen's toll is now counted in malnutrition and cut roads rather than in airport dead; Sudan's is counted in darkened operating theatres and 55 dead medics.
