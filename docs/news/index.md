    **The numbers behind it are not ambiguous, and one of them is almost a definition of the crisis.** The average rent in Spain has **almost doubled in ten years**; it now stands at about **€1,176 a month**, which the **Youth Council of Spain** calculates is equivalent to roughly **98.7% of a young person's average monthly salary**. The **Bank of Spain** puts the housing deficit at about **750,000 homes**, rising past **one million by 2028**. Last month **parliament rejected** the government's decrees aimed at curbing evictions and limiting rent pressure — the rejection that led Prime Minister **Pedro Sánchez** to call a snap election for **29 November**, eighteen days after the strike. The unions want a new minimum-wage agreement and more state intervention in the housing market; the tenants' associations want tourist apartments eliminated, speculation curbed and leases renewed automatically. Sánchez's Socialists trail in the polls, with corruption cases involving his inner circle and a second fatal rail crash this year — **69 killed**, a state funeral — in the background, and Madrid's teachers have been on strike for nearly a month.

    **The day itself was large, uneven, and wet.** The unions reported early participation of **90%** at ports and in the automotive sector — total stoppages at **Volkswagen Navarra** and **SEAT Barcelona** — **85%** in the meat industry, **82%** in waste collection and **75%** in energy, against **38%** in health and social services and **35%** in the postal service. Madrid's metro ran at 80–90% of peak trains with roughly half the usual passengers. Pickets blocked roads with burning tyres in Barcelona. Two storms, **Charles** and **Bruna**, pushed **AEMET** to issue alerts for **14 regions**, threatening the afternoon marches.

## The Pattern

Look across these five — **an arrangements committee seated before a government exists**, **a second day of talks with a table and no paper**, **a blockade extended from the sea to the land and left without a rulebook**, **one hundred and twenty signatures that must become a committee before they mean anything**, and **a general strike eighteen days before a ballot** — and the structure of this week is not a decision. It is a **hand-off**.

**In each case the authority has already moved and the document has not.** Sixty-seven seats give the anti-Netanyahu bloc the agenda, and the inquiry it demanded at the opening session has no terms of reference, no chair and no reporting date. Abu Dhabi has the delegations, the venue, the envoys and its last scheduled day — and no published text, while the refinery campaign produces the only numbers the week can agree on. Washington has replaced the strike with an interdiction regime of twenty people in Florida and no published standard for what is inspected or what may pass. Jakarta has 120 signatures, a vice-president who offered to resign in private, and a special committee that must be formed before the petition becomes a proceeding. Madrid has a ballot date and a 750,000-home deficit with no published completion schedule — but it does have a rent figure, and that figure is why the country stopped.

**The tell is what each one is counting, and what each one has stopped counting.** In Jerusalem the count is signatures on a request, and the vote they bought is on the calendar for 17 November. In Abu Dhabi the count is refineries — nine of 35, thirty per cent of capacity — and the talks have no annex for it. In the Gulf the count is tonnage through a blockade whose rules are unwritten, and a tanker was hit while the rules stayed unwritten. In Jakarta the count is hectares, and the ministry's own series has been frozen at July's 202,000 while independent monitors report 637,000 and nearly 900,000; the same government's approval has fallen from 81.2% to 44.5%, which is the cost of a stopped count. In Spain the count is euros against salary — 98.7% — and that single ratio did what no report could.

**What this does to causality is precise, and it is the part that matters here.** A transferred authority that has not yet acted is the one moment in a political cycle when a cause can still be changed cheaply, because no position has been spent yet. That window closes on a published date in each case: 17 November for the speaker, the end of the round in Abu Dhabi, the ministerial decision that writes the blockade's rules, the formation of the Indonesian special committee, and 29 November in Spain. §110's instruction is operational rather than moral — change the cause toward the good and the effect changes — but a cause can only be changed by a body that has the power to act, in the days before it acts. The instrument in each of these five files already exists. What does not exist is the paragraph that says what it will do, and who signs it.

**The rest of the ledger.** **China**'s surplus stays where it was — $1.04 trillion for January–October, Xi and Trump due at **APEC in Shenzhen on 18–19 November** — with no new number today, so it leaves the five and keeps its slot in the file. **Senegal** holds: the Atlantic route's toll stands at 39 dead from one boat and **El Hierro**'s emergency remains in force, but nothing verifiable moved in twenty-four hours, and a repeated toll is not a new cause. **Gaza** is in the ledger twice over: flooding in the tent camps killed at least **four** from one family, thousands lost shelters, an Israeli strike hit a tent in Gaza City on Wednesday, Israel says Gaza will not be rebuilt until **Hamas** disarms, and the **Board of Peace** calls Israel's aid response *"insufficient"* — a reconstruction veto held against a disarmament that has not happened, in the season when the weather is doing the demolition. **Haiti** produced the week's sharpest image: **El Salvador**'s military, in country since last month with more than **2,000** soldiers on a mission to recover four abducted technicians, struck the **400 Mawozo** headquarters outside Port-au-Prince by air and ground, with up to **20** killed reported and unconfirmed, the gang's leader reportedly absent, and **Amnesty International** asking that Salvadoran operations comply with human-rights law — against a UN count of at least **5,700** killed or injured by gangs or in operations against them between 1 January and 11 September. **COP31** moved to the leaders' segment in Antalya with the hosts under fire and **1.47°C** for January–September as the number it will be judged against; **Novaya Gazeta**, Russia's last independent newspaper of consequence, closed its office after three decades; and the **Nvidia** affair and **Kenya**'s El Niño warning remain open files without slots.
