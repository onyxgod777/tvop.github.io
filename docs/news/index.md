### 🔥 The Strike Landed in the Financial District

???+ danger "Observed Cause"

### 🤖 The Router Sent a Thousand Hellos Every Four Seconds

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    **By Saturday the explanation had changed twice, and the second version is worse than the first.** On Friday afternoon a contractor, **L3Harris**, was replacing copper cabling with fibre-optic cable in a decades-old aviation telecoms network — the copper was so old it was being discontinued — and at a remote site it installed a **router**. According to **Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy**, that router began *"flooding"* the other systems in the air traffic control network, sending about **1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds**. **Bryan Bedford**, the FAA administrator, said **17 of the 22 air route traffic control centres** in the United States experienced instability in voice and ground communications. At **15:54 EST** every departing flight in the continental US was stopped; the order held for more than four hours, the longest such stoppage since **11 September 2001**.

    **The most important number in this story is forty-five minutes, and what happened during it.** It took the FAA that long to find where the problem was, Bedford admitted, *because maintenance is logged manually on paper* — so staff had to *"pore through"* physical documents *"to figure out who's working on what."* The agency that regulates a continent's airspace could not answer, electronically, which contractor was working on which component. Duffy said the government is now trying to establish whether the fault was *"incompetence"* on the part of the company doing the upgrades or faulty equipment, and that there would be a *"re-evaluation"* of allowing upgrades during the day; **L3Harris** said it had completed *"more than 70%"* of its modernisation work before the shutdown, takes the impact *"seriously"*, and is supporting the investigation. **Bedford**: *"zero evidence of anything nefarious and zero evidence of anything AI related."* More than **15,000** flights were delayed or cancelled on Friday and a further **5,900 cancelled** and **7,000 delayed** on Saturday, with about **1.5 million** passengers affected; the FAA says the schedule should be normal by the end of Sunday. **Trump** has requested **\\$30.1bn**; Duffy puts the real figure at **\\$50bn**; both chambers of Congress are held by the president's party. Aviation analyst **Miles O'Brien** called the system *"a house that's about to be condemned."*

### 🇺🇦 The Guarantee Has a Throttle Now

???+ danger "Observed Cause"

### 🗳️ The First Election Is Now the Third Postponement

???+ danger "Observed Cause"
    **South Sudan has never held a national election. On Saturday it postponed one again.** The country's electoral commission announced that the vote scheduled for **December** will now take place on **11 February 2027** — the latest in a series of delays stretching back to independence in **2011**, and the second postponement of this particular poll. The decision came days after **UN envoys warned** that holding the election next month risked unravelling the fragile peace, and months after **President Salva Kiir dissolved the government** in September, ahead of the vote. Legal analysts in the region immediately asked the question the announcement left open: *whether the delay itself is lawful* — under what provision of the **2018 peace agreement**, on whose authority, and with what consequence for the transitional arrangements that were supposed to expire.

    **This is not a story about an election being late. It is a story about the only mechanism that could have made a government accountable being exchanged for stability — and both sides of that exchange being unverifiable.** The envoys' argument was substantive: a vote held in December, in a country where renewed fighting has displaced hundreds of thousands, where armed groups control whole districts, and where the register of voters has never been tested, could produce a result that half the country rejects — and rejection is how the last civil war began. The counter-argument is equally substantive: every postponement entrenches the incumbency that the vote was meant to test, and a government that has dissolved itself and reconstituted itself without an election has already demonstrated what it does with the interval. Both arguments are correct. Which one prevails is being decided by the same small group of people whose tenure is the subject of the decision.

??? warning "Projected Effect (if cause persists)"

## The Pattern

Look across these five — **a drone that arrived over Riyadh's financial district in the week the war was declared over**, **a router that sent a thousand connection attempts every four seconds and a maintenance log kept on paper**, **a satellite link throttled over territory the guarantor does not recognise as Russian**, **a climate summit whose final week is being spent on a publication date**, and **a first election postponed again with no instrument cited** — and the structure of the week is not a failure of any one institution. It is **the decision that has been made and not written down**.

**In each case the operative act was taken without the document that would make it reviewable.** The United States declared the Iran war militarily over, and a missile hit an Aramco tower in the Saudi capital; no damage assessment, no interception record, no deconfliction channel exists to test what "over" means. The FAA grounded a continent for four hours and could not say for forty-five minutes who was working on what, because maintenance is recorded by hand. A vendor narrowed a weapons system's coverage over allied ground, and the author of the order has not been named — while Washington and Kyiv give different accounts of who that author is. Antalya will produce a cover decision on Friday that can praise the IPCC while leaving its timetable to further consultations, so no party will own the delay. And Juba moved its first election by fourteen weeks without citing the provision, the authority, or the court that permitted it.

**The missing artifact is the same in all five: a named author and a date.** Not a register this time — a signature. Who ordered the restriction; who approved maintenance during operating hours; who instructed the wording that leaves the calendar open; who signed the postponement and under what clause; who certified that the war had ended and on what evidence. Each of these is a one-line fact. Each is the fact that converts a decision into something a legislature, a court or a voter could examine — which is why none is published, and why, in every case, the party withholding it is the party that benefits from the ambiguity.

**What this does to causality is precise.** §10:49 says a promise is carried out *if you are truthful*; §9:100 says authority comes from a people's choosing; §28:38 says responsibility is pushed onto *"next ones or other fellow human beings"*; §2:351 forbids being *stirred up* to war by leaders. All four point at one mechanism: the cause of each of this week's five is a decision whose author is unnamed, and an unnamed cause cannot be changed — you cannot alter what nobody will admit to having set. The effect of that pattern is not collapse. It is **the transfer of decision-making to whoever is least accountable for it**: a router in a remote facility, a vendor's coverage policy, a paragraph left to further consultations, an electoral commission's announcement, a president's rhetorical aside. Each is small enough to disown. Together they are the government of the world.
